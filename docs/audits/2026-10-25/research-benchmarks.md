# Roadmap benchmarks — 2026-10-25
Training/testing and container workloads only; physical production/ISO excluded. **Verified** means primary documentation, not product testing. **Inferred** marks recommendations; no containd code maturity is asserted.

## 1. Secure remote access (priority)

**Verified benchmarks.** [Claroty xDome Secure Access (formerly SRA)](https://www.claroty.com/platform/secure-access): PAM, vendor access, approvals/live oversight. [Cyolo](https://docs.cyolo.io/docs/policy-actions): approvals, duration limits, recording, SSH command control. [XONA](https://www.xonasystems.com/resource/xona-secure-access-for-critical-infrastructure): browser/pixel protocol isolation; its “eliminating ransomware risk” is marketing, not assurance. [Secomea](https://secomea.com/remote-access/): per-machine rights, SSO/MFA, approval, supervision/termination, scanned transfers. Its [recording limits](https://kb.secomea.com/docs/session-recording): Professional/Premium, browser RDP/VNC/SSH/Telnet, 90-day retention.

[Tosibox](https://www.tosibox.com/hubfs/Tosibox%20User%20Manual%202025.pdf): trusted-device two-factor VPN; do not equate VPN logs with video or infer approvals. [Dispel](https://dispel.com/features): JIT, vendor onboarding, MFA, recording, file scanning, SIEM; [outbound-only site connector](https://help.dispel.io/en/articles/9527227-system-components-responsibility). [BeyondTrust OT](https://www.beyondtrust.com/solutions/operational-technology): application-scoped Jump access, credentials, JIT, recording, clipboard restriction.

Firewall ≠ SRA/PAM: [FortiPAM](https://www.fortinet.com/products/fortipam) supplies credential/session controls (FortiSRA URL now redirects here). [Palo Alto Prisma Browser PRA](https://docs.paloaltonetworks.com/prisma-browser/administration/prisma-browser-remote-connections): controlled RDP/SSH/VNC, live oversight. [Cisco SEA](https://www.cisco.com/c/en/us/products/collateral/security/industrial-security/secure-equipment-access/sec-equipment-access-ds.html): outbound broker, per-asset schedules/protocols, MFA/approval on supported switches/routers; not synonymous with ISA3000 VPN.

**Standards/guidance.** [ISA 3-3 contents](https://www.isa.org/getmedia/4379f3a1-2687-4a0a-9d0f-20d743233c1b/ISA-62443-3-3_TOC.pdf): SR 1.13 untrusted-network access; 2.6 remote termination; 5.1/5.2 segmentation/boundaries; 5.3 person-to-person restrictions; 5.4 application partitioning. Control intent: monitor/control external access, explicit approval (1.13 RE1), idle/user termination, controlled conduits. [ISA mapping](https://isagca.org/hubfs/2023%20ISA%20Website%20Redesigns/ISAGCA/PDFs/NERC%20CIP%2062443_1124_FINAL.pdf): 5.2 RE1 default-deny, RE2 island, RE3 fail-close. Intent mapping is not licensed clause/security-level verification; confirm before compliance claims. Outbound-only and video recording are not universal 3-3 mandates.

[NIST 800-82r3](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.SP.800-82r3) §§6.2.10, F/AC-17: justify/limit access, secure connections, consider MFA/temporary windows; train safe disconnection without disrupting OT. [NSA/CISA 2020](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/cybersecurity-advisories/aa20-205a): reduce exposure, secure approved accounts, two-factor remote authentication. [Joint 2023 guide](https://www.cisa.gov/resources-tools/resources/guide-securing-remote-access-software): remote-tool abuse/mitigation. Both advisory full-text fetches returned 403; summaries use indexed official excerpts. Guidance is not automatically a legal obligation.

**Inferred checklist / labs.** Brokered access; outbound connector/no unsolicited OT inbound ports (broker still listens); default-deny per-asset/protocol RBAC; JIT expiry, approval/reason, MFA; live monitoring/termination/recording; isolated RDP/VNC/SSH/HTTPS; controlled clipboard/uploads/downloads with quarantine; named vendor sponsor/expiry/offboarding; protected identity→approval→policy→session/file audit and retention. All teach well: deny a second PLC, expire/kill an active session, quarantine a transfer, replay/export incident evidence. **Now:** IdP + existing proxy integration. **Next:** approvals, expiry, termination, evidence. **Later:** vault/posture integration. Test broker/IdP/recorder outages and audited break-glass, not silent bypass.

## 2. Mature industrial firewall features

**Verified product families (capabilities vary by firmware/license).**
- [Hirschmann EAGLE40-4F](https://www.belden.com/products/industrial-networking-cybersecurity/cybersecurity/firewalls/eagle40-4f-security): stateful routed/transparent filtering, learning, IPsec, audit/RBAC and DPI enforcers for Modbus TCP, OPC, EtherNet/IP, IEC104 and DNP3. [EAGLE family](https://www.belden.com/products/industrial-networking-cybersecurity/cybersecurity/firewalls/eagle40) adds VRRP and Industrial HiVision management. [Tofino](https://www.belden.com/about/connected-brands/tofino) is a historical modular enforcer benchmark, not a current procurement default: all remaining products discontinued, orders ended December 2025; support ends April 2031. Belden points to IAF-240.
- [Moxa EDR-G9010](https://www.moxa.com/en/products/industrial-network-infrastructure/network-security-appliance/edr-g9010-series): firewall/NAT/router/VPN, industrial DPI, IDS/IPS, MXsecurity and ring redundancy. [EDF-G1002-BP](https://moxa.com/en/products/industrial-network-infrastructure/network-security-appliance/edf-g1002-bp-series): transparent bump-in-wire, monitor/protect modes, granular command DPI, IPS/virtual patching, bypass and central updates. Ring redundancy or bypass must not be mislabeled state-synchronized firewall HA.
- [Cisco ISA3000](https://www.cisco.com/c/en/us/products/collateral/security/industrial-security-appliance-3000/data-sheet-c78-735839.html): routed/transparent policy, command/value inspection, NGIPS, passive discovery/learning, central management, VPN, active/standby failover. Lists Modbus, S7, CIP/EtherNet/IP, DNP3, IEC104, MMS, OPC-UA and others; “visibility and/or control” is not equal enforcement depth for every protocol.
- [FortiGate Rugged](https://www.fortinet.com/content/dam/fortinet/assets/data-sheets/FortiGate_Rugged_Series.pdf): zone/conduit segmentation, OT application/payload DPI, IPS/virtual patching, VPN, HA, FortiManager/FortiAnalyzer integration. Subscription/content and model limits matter more than headline protocol counts.
- [Palo Alto industrial OT](https://www.paloaltonetworks.com/network-security/ot-security-solution): App-ID/Device-ID, inventory, least-privilege policy recommendations and threat prevention. [Functional App-ID example](https://knowledgebase.paloaltonetworks.com/KCSArticleDetail?id=kA10g000000ClZ9CAK) documents ICCP function variants: benchmark command semantics, not just port recognition.
- [OPNsense IPS](https://docs.opnsense.org/manual/ips.html) and [pfSense add-ons](https://docs.netgate.com/pfsense/en/latest/packages/snort/index.html): Suricata/Snort-based inspection, not a verified turnkey ICS-policy suite. [Suricata rules](https://docs.suricata.io/en/suricata-8.0.4/rules/) include Modbus, DNP3 and ENIP/CIP keywords; deployed engine, parser enablement and rule feeds determine actual coverage. OPNsense documents EVE/syslog export; [pfSense HA](https://docs.netgate.com/pfsense/en/latest/recipes/high-availability.html) uses CARP/pfsync. These are FreeBSD firewalls, not ordinary Linux containers.

**Inferred recurring checklist.** Zone/conduit default-deny policy; L3/L4 state plus industrial command/object allowlists; observe→review→enforce workflow; transparent/routed/NAT options; VPN; IDS/IPS with update lifecycle; passive assets/flows; RBAC/central config; searchable decisions and SIEM export; backup/rollback; HA and explicit failure behavior. Not every appliance implements every item. Learning must propose rules, never automatically trust an attack. Separate protocol identification, semantic enforcement and exploit detection in benchmark scores.

**Roadmap inference. Now:** reproducible learn/review/enforce labs, read-vs-write controls, reasoned event export. **Next:** passive inventory, policy diff/rollback, API-driven multi-instance management and IDS integration. **Later:** virtual HA/failure drills. Do not spend this roadmap on rugged hardware, physical bypass certification or ISO packaging.

## 3. Protecting containerized workloads

**Verified deployment patterns.**
- **Docker host/plugin:** [network-driver plugins](https://docs.docker.com/engine/extend/plugins_network/) extend LibNetwork; a driver is not inherently policy. [Docker firewall rules](https://docs.docker.com/engine/network/packet-filtering-firewalls/) implement bridge isolation/NAT; published ports can bypass ufw. [iptables](https://docs.docker.com/engine/network/firewall-iptables/) provides DOCKER-USER for pre-Docker forwarding policy; [native nftables](https://docs.docker.com/engine/network/firewall-nftables/) has no DOCKER-USER, instead separate tables/hooks/priorities. Docker 29 native nftables remains experimental, not Swarm-capable. Preserve Docker-owned rules.
- **Compose gateway:** [networks](https://docs.docker.com/reference/compose-file/networks/) control membership, not industrial commands; [service network mode](https://docs.docker.com/reference/compose-file/services/#network_mode) can share a gateway's namespace. [Compose lifecycle](https://docs.docker.com/compose/how-tos/networking/) retains service names but changes addresses on replacement. A firewall on a shared bridge is not necessarily inline (**inferred**).
- **Kubernetes CNI:** [NetworkPolicy](https://kubernetes.io/docs/concepts/services-networking/network-policies/) needs an enforcing plugin; absent policies normally leave ingress/egress unrestricted. [Calico](https://docs.tigera.io/calico/latest/reference/resources/networkpolicy) uses ordered selector-based endpoint rules with namespace/service-account criteria. [Cilium](https://docs.cilium.io/en/stable/security/policy/layer7/) adds proxied HTTP/DNS application policy to L3/L4. Neither demonstrates native ICS command filtering. NetworkPolicy alone does not insert containd.
- **Sidecar/gateway proxy:** [Istio security guidance](https://istio.io/latest/docs/ops/best-practices/security/) warns against treating a workload's own outbound sidecar as an unconditional security boundary; combine a gateway with NetworkPolicy to enforce the path. This is not ICS DPI.
- **nftables inside a container:** [network namespaces](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man7/network_namespaces.7.html) isolate routes/firewall rules; rules in an ordinary container do not govern all host traffic. [Docker capabilities](https://docs.docker.com/engine/containers/run/#runtime-privilege-and-linux-capabilities) allow scoped NET_ADMIN instead of blanket privileged mode. [NFQUEUE](https://wiki.nftables.org/wiki-nftables/index.php/Queueing_to_userspace) sends packets to a userspace verdict engine: no listener normally drops; `bypass` permits traversal when absent. Listener loss ≠ overflow.

**Inferred minimum useful ICS firewall container.** An unavoidable inspected path: two disjoint workload networks joined only through a routed/bridged gateway, or explicit application proxies; test same-host peers, published ports, IPv6, alternate interfaces and direct addresses for bypass. Default-deny conduits plus protocol/function/object policy; bounded TCP reassembly and explicit malformed/unknown/encrypted-traffic handling (ciphertext cannot provide command semantics). Dynamic workload-name/label→IP mapping with stale-identity cleanup; immutable policy revisions and atomic apply/rollback. Separate management/SRA plane; least capabilities, no workload Docker socket, pinned images/config persistence. Expose policy decisions, drops, queue saturation, parser limits and latency; define startup/readiness, crash/restart and fail-open/closed behavior.

**Roadmap inference. Now:** a documented Compose reference topology and automated read-allowed/write-denied + bypass/crash tests. **Next:** identity reconciliation, metrics/evidence, lifecycle/failure tests and Linux/backend matrix (Desktop separately). **Later:** a CNI-compatible gateway/traffic-steering integration alongside Cilium/Calico, not a replacement CNI. Validate real packet paths before any Kubernetes protection claim. Host-root/cluster-admin compromise remains outside a container-only firewall boundary.
